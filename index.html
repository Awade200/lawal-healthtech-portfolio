01 / SULAWOG CLINIC

Designing the clinic around the patient journey.

HealthTechProduct operationsClinical workflow

The clinic needed more than a patient list. It needed a reliable way to follow someone from first contact through consultation, treatment, payment and continued care — while giving each staff member the information they actually needed.

My roleOperations Manager with product responsibility
Worked withCEO · clinic team · front desk · therapists · engineer · users
What matteredContinuity · usability · records · visibility
The problem

Before the system, patient activity was not documented consistently enough to make follow-up or management analysis dependable. It was difficult to keep tabs on patients after a visit, trace who referred them, or build a clear picture of what happened across the full clinic journey.

That also made growth harder to understand. If someone came through a recommendation, there was no dependable referral trail connecting the referrer, the new patient and any reward or future follow-up.

Registration→Walk-in / appointment→Check-in→Consultation→Vitals & notes→Prescription / therapy→Billing→Payment→Checkout→Follow-up
A decision that changed

The first version was shaped too heavily around appointment booking. In practice, staff and patients were not behaving that way — the clinic handled far more walk-ins.

We changed the flow rather than forcing users to fit the original assumption. Walk-in registration and check-in became part of the normal path. This was one of the moments that reinforced how important it was to watch the operation after launch, not just define it before build.

01

Start with the operation

I mapped what actually happened from reception through consultation, treatment, billing and follow-up before turning the stages into product requirements.

02

Keep the patient history connected

A returning patient should not start again. Visits, notes, vitals, therapy, prescriptions and billing needed to remain attached to the patient record.

03

Design around different users

Reception, clinical staff, therapy, accounting and management do not need the same view. The workflow had to support responsibility without exposing unnecessary complexity.

04

Measure what management needs

The system was designed to make patient activity, referral source, service use, payments, outstanding balances and growth trends easier to analyse.

What changed

Clinic information moved toward a single patient-centred operating flow rather than disconnected pieces of documentation. Staff could work from the same underlying record, while management gained clearer visibility into patient activity and the commercial side of the clinic.

The project also created a better foundation for the next questions: Which referral sources bring people in? Where do patients drop out? Who needs follow-up? Which services are being used? Those are the questions I want the product to make easier to answer.

02 / SULAWOG

Understanding what was driving growth — and building the operation around it.

GrowthProduct operationsData

Growth was not just a marketing question. I needed to understand demand, visibility, enquiries, conversion, production and accounting together.

ProductDemand · product mix · pricing · availability
AcquisitionVisibility · referrals · enquiries · partnerships
ConversionEnquiry → consultation / purchase → follow-up
CommercialAccounting · revenue trends · outstanding balances
How I approached it

I worked across clinic, product, sales, marketing and accounting rather than treating growth as a single-channel problem. We began putting clearer enquiry and follow-up structures in place, tying marketing activity to meaningful actions such as consultations, clinic visits and purchases.

A referral model also became part of the growth direction: capture who referred a new patient, then make it possible to recognise the referrer with an agreed clinic benefit such as a check-up or promotional service. The important part is the tracking — without that, the programme cannot be measured or managed properly.

Impact reporting: I am keeping exact growth figures off this public version until the underlying accounting periods are reconciled. The portfolio will use verified ranges or percentage change rather than impressive-looking numbers that cannot be defended.
03 / SULAWOG

Making the organisation easier to control as it grew.

Internal systemsInventoryProcess

The operational question was simple: can management tell what we have, where it is, what moved, what sold, what is outstanding and what needs attention without rebuilding the picture manually?

Demand→Production / stock-in→Location→Sale / transfer→Accounting→Reporting
The system behind the work

I helped define operational workflows for inventory, locations, movements, customer balances, corrections and management reporting. The aim was not to create another dashboard; it was to reduce the amount of organisational knowledge that lived only in people's heads or separate records.

User feedback changed this work too. The control centre went through several iterations as staff needs became clearer — particularly around how stock movement, account balances and corrections should be represented.
