01 / HealthTech Product Sulawog Clinic Management System

Designing the clinic around the full patient journey.

I worked from inside Sulawog operations to turn the real clinic flow into a digital system — from registration and walk-in check-in through consultation, treatment, billing and follow-up.

My roleOperations Manager with product responsibility
Worked withCEO · Engineer · Front desk · Clinic team · Accounting · Users
FocusWorkflow · Records · Billing · Follow-up · Analytics
Register→Walk-in / Book→Check in→Consult→Vitals & notes→Prescription / therapy→Bill & pay→Checkout→Follow-up

The problem

There was no reliable way to keep the patient journey together.

Before the system, documentation was inconsistent and follow-up depended too much on people remembering what happened. It was difficult to keep tabs on returning patients, understand referral sources, or connect clinical activity with the wider operating picture.

What I did

I mapped the work before defining the product.

I broke the clinic into the real jobs people needed to complete, drafted the workflow and requirements, discussed priorities with management and clinic users, and worked with the engineer to review, test and refine the implementation.

A decision that changed

Appointments looked right on paper. Walk-ins matched reality.

The first version leaned too heavily on appointment booking. Usage showed that the clinic handled far more walk-ins. We changed the flow so a patient could arrive, be found or registered, checked in and moved through care without forcing an appointment-first model.

What became clearer

Patient history, status and next action could live in one workflow.

The system created a clearer operating view across patient records, visits, consultation notes, vitals, therapy, prescriptions, billing and payment. It also gave management a better base for tracking activity, referrals and follow-up.

What I learned

Operational software gets better when real use is allowed to challenge the original assumptions. The walk-in change is the clearest example: the product had to follow the clinic, not force the clinic to follow the product.

Next iteration

Referral-source tracking, structured follow-up, care continuity and incentive logic are natural extensions because they connect patient experience with retention and growth.
